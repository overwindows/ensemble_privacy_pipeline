Key Takeaway — How Much Comes from Concurrency/KV Env Vars vs. Fast Binary Search + bt=32768?
1. On 120-prompt suites (2.5% overlong truncation): Env vars alone (Change 1) provide the largest initial jump (+50.4% on v2 120p and +87.6% on v1 120p), while Changes 2+3 (Fast Single-Encode Binary Search + bt=32768) add another +35% to +68% over stock—bringing total throughput to 33.64–37.87 QPS with cache disabled and 39.18–39.62 QPS (2.19×–2.31×) with full stack, while cutting p95 tail latency by 31%–36%.
2. Both layers are required to eliminate host-CPU lock stalls under multilingual bursts.

Section 3 Diagnosis at a Glance: Why Stock Production Plateaus at 13.8–18.1 QPS

Inspecting /model/model/ inside cliff-freshness-dlis-falcon:20261021-v2 alongside 1 Hz nvidia-smi telemetry proves that the A100 GPU sits idle 22%–47% of the time waiting on host admission and CPU tokenization:

Bottleneck 1: Concurrency Caps Config

http_server.py defaults to _Parallelism_=1 and model.py:224–225 defaults to VLLM_MAX_NUM_SEQS="4", VLLM_GPU_MEMORY_UTILIZATION="0.80". Even with 32 callers, vLLM only batches 4 sequences.

Bottleneck 2: 37-Pass BSearch Lock Code

In model.py:307, prepare_request() runs inside with self._tok_lock:. Overlong articles (>8,192 tokens) run a 17-step binary search in freshness_prompt.py that encodes both prompt and dummy assistant turn at every step (37 full ~8k-token passes ≈ 280 ms locked CPU).

Bottleneck 3: Hardcoded bt=8320 Code

In model.py:234, max_num_batched_tokens=8320 is hardcoded to a single sequence's context limit, preventing vLLM from batching more than ~2 concurrent ~3,600-token prefills per step.
