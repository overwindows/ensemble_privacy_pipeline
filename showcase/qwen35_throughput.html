Executive Summary. On the full 2,000-request production evaluation benchmark (l1_v2_gpt56_test.jsonl, 5,871,663 prompt tokens, 2,574,218 output tokens) served over online HTTP /v1/chat/completions on a single NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB (vLLM 0.31.0 V2 Model Runner), our Wave-6g 4-Patch Greedy Champion (magenta_book_0g0hp7t2cy) achieves 3,879.50 output tok/s (12,728.44 total tok/s, 3.01 QPS, 663.54s wall time, 2000/2000 = 100.0% valid JSON, and 4,040.4 tok/s steady-state median at Running=128). All four patches are pure Python/Triton, apply in <1s at container boot with zero C++ rebuild, and are committed to GitHub for one-command reproduction.

4.1  Kernel, CUDA Graph & Sampling Optimizations on `vLLM 0.31.0`

We systematically profiled the vLLM 0.31.0 V2 GPU worker on 1× A100-80GB and evaluated every candidate kernel and graph optimization across full 2,000-request online HTTP runs:

  • Why vllm_v031_fast_spec_sample.patch saves 2.03 GB/step (+31.04 tok/s): In stock vLLM 0.31.0, RejectionSampler.forward unconditionally calls apply_sampling_params(..., logits.to(torch.float32)) on the [512, 248320] target vocabulary tensor (127.1M elements) and runs a separate _temperature_kernel before rejection_sample_kernel — even when no top_k, top_p < 1.0, or penalty processors are active and even though rejection_sample_kernel already supports inline apply_temperature. Passing BF16 logits zero-copy eliminates 2.03 GB/step (88%) of rejection-sampling HBM traffic.
  • Why vllm_v031_eagle_full_cudagraph.patch unlocks draft FULL_DECODE_ONLY CUDA graphs (+23.13 tok/s greedy / +31.26 tok/s DSpark): Because the target model uses FLASHINFER alongside 24 Mamba/GDN layers, vLLM downgrades top-level cudagraph_mode to PIECEWISE. Stock AutoRegressiveSpeculator.set_cudagraph_mode only enables FULL_DECODE_ONLY when the target's decode mode is FULL, forcing all 3 MTP draft decode steps to execute piecewise through Python. Decoupling the draft check when draft_attn_backend == "TRITON_ATTN" captures all 3 draft steps into a single CUDA graph per batch size.
  • Why 2-hunk vllm_v031_gdn_minimal.patch beat the 4-hunk Python mixed-batch split (+2.04 tok/s vs. -17.30 tok/s): During the ~4,000 mixed prefill+decode steps, splitting decode and prefill tokens in Python adds 264 extra CUDA kernel launches per step (torch.index_select + index_copy_ across 24 GDN layers), costing more CPU/launch latency than it saves on GPU. Keeping only the zero-overhead torch.zeros → torch.empty fix on the fused C++ GDN decode path lifted the 3-patch stack from 3,877.46 to 3,879.50 output tok/s.
  • Why Native MTP k=3 beats k=4 (+132.51 tok/s): Increasing speculative depth from k=3 to k=4 (calm_panda_v0f2cf9v30) adds a 4th draft step with marginal acceptance (0.556 on position 3, lowering overall acceptance from 76.64% → 71.13%) and swells the target verification batch from 4N=512 to 5N=640 tokens/step (+25% target FLA/attention compute), dropping throughput from 3,879.50 to 3,746.99 tok/s.

4.2  Speculator Architecture Comparison: Native 1L MTP (`3,879.50`) vs. External 2L DSpark (`3,738.36`) vs. 4L DFlash (`1,608.43`)

We evaluated three distinct speculative decoding architectures on the same Qwen3.5-4B backbone:

  1. Native 1-Layer MTP (Qwen3_5MTP, k=3 — Winner at 3,879.50 tok/s): Built directly into the checkpoint, shares target backbone hidden states, reads only 1 decoder layer of weights per draft step, and achieves 76.64% acceptance (3.30 tokens/step) in ~18.0 ms/step at Running=128.
  2. External 2-Layer DSpark (IWRITECODE/qwen35-l1-dspark-2l-v32k, k=3 — Best at 3,738.36 tok/s): Out-of-the-box, vLLM 0.31.0 fragmented the hybrid KV cache into 34 groups (2,175.87 tok/s). Fixing the KV grouping (34 → 4 groups, +68.2%), enabling FULL_DECODE_ONLY draft CUDA graphs, and running greedy decoding lifted DSpark to 3,738.36 tok/s with slightly higher acceptance (78.76%, 3.36 tokens/step). However, because DSpark runs 2 full transformer layers + a 32,768-vocab Markov head per draft step (~2× the draft HBM weight traffic of 1-layer MTP), its +0.06 tokens/step acceptance advantage (+1.8%) cannot offset its +4.7% step-time cost (~18.85 ms vs ~18.0 ms). We also tested k=5 (3,471.93 tok/s, 768-token verification bottleneck) and pruning the Markov head to top-64 (gray_drop_m1pb1vf7v9, 3,695.43 tok/s, where 500× Markov bandwidth reduction was offset by -1.49 pp acceptance loss when bigram boosts fell in ranks 65–256).
  3. External 4-Layer DFlash (z-lab/Qwen3.5-4B-DFlash, 1.71B params — Closed at 1,608.43 tok/s): Block-diffusion drafting adds 4 full layers of weight reads and dedicated cross-attention KV cache pressure every step, running 33.0% slower than native MTP at c=128.

5  Cross-Harness Calibration: Resolving the `2,697.54` vs. `3,786.84` Gap

Early in the campaign, offline runs via benchmarks/gemma4_moe_benchmarks/run_config.py reported 2,697.54 output tok/s — 28.8% below r9 (3,786.84 tok/s). Forensic line-by-line audit uncovered five concrete harness discrepancies. Fixing all five closed 100% of the gap (lifting offline throughput from 2,697.54 → 3,335.90 → 3,853.40 output tok/s, +42.8%) before we transitioned to the online HTTP /v1/chat/completions server harness:

  1. Missing enable_thinking=False + Hardcoded T=0.7 Sampling (+638.36 tok/s / +23.7% when fixed): run_config.py omitted chat_template_kwargs={"enable_thinking": False} and hardcoded temperature=0.7, top_p=0.95, causing Qwen3.5-4B to emit verbose <think>...</think> blocks that inflated mean output length by +74.4% (1,291 → 2,252 tok/req).
  2. vLLM Runtime Version (v0.1.dev1 V1 Fork vs. vLLM 0.31.0 V2 Model Runner, +517.50 tok/s / +15.5% when fixed): r9 ran on vLLM 0.31.0 V2 Model Runner (with CUDA GDN decode kernels and FULL_AND_PIECEWISE CUDA graphs), whereas the older fork fell back to PIECEWISE graphs and Triton GDN.
  3. Target vs. Draft Attention Backend Split: Forwarding attention_backend="FLASHINFER" on the 8 target layers while keeping TRITON_ATTN inside speculative_config for the 1-layer MTP head avoided the FLASH_ATTN head_dim=256 penalty (-34%).
  4. BF16 Mamba State Cache & Language-Model-Only Loading: Forwarding mamba_ssm_cache_dtype="bfloat16" and language_model_only=True matched r9's exact 53.79 GiB KV/Mamba cache allocation.

6  Path to 4,000+ End-to-End tok/s & References

With the Wave-6g 4-patch greedy champion at 3,879.50 output tok/s end-to-end across 2,000 requests — and 4,040.4 tok/s median throughput already achieved during steady-state Running=128 pure-decode windows — only 120.50 tok/s (+3.11%) of ramp/drain and mixed-step amortization remains to cross 4,000+ tok/s end-to-end on 1× A100-80GB:

  • C++-level mixed-batch GDN decode dispatch: Extending ops.fused_gdn_decode_post_conv_mtp in C++/CUDA to accept mixed prefill+decode index masks directly (eliminating the ~4,000 mixed-step fallback to the Triton chunked FLA prefill kernel without Python tensor slicing) targets the remaining ~120 tok/s gap between the 3,879.50 tok/s end-to-end average and the 4,040.4 tok/s steady-state decode median.
  • Weight quantization (for 4,200–5,000+ tok/s stretch goal): Applying W8A8 FP8/INT8 SmoothQuant or W4A16 Marlin quantization to the 24 GDN + 8 full-attention target layers cuts per-step weight HBM reads by 50–75% on memory-bandwidth-bound A100 decode steps.
